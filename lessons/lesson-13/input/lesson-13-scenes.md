## Scene 01

[natural pace] [interest] [natural]
Hi, and welcome back to Act. You already have a clearer solution from the last unit. Now the question changes: how would that solution become something you could actually carry out? A good idea is not yet a plan. Planning means turning the idea into clear work: what needs to happen, in what order, what each step needs, and when it should be done. [short pause] The goal is not to make the plan complicated. The goal is to make the next action obvious.

## Scene 02

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [natural]
One of the easiest planning mistakes is keeping the project too big in your head. “Run an awareness initiative” sounds clear, but it is still too large to act on. A useful plan breaks a project into main parts, then breaks each part into smaller tasks. [short pause] Think of it like opening a box and finding smaller boxes inside it. The project is the big box. The main steps are the smaller boxes. The tasks inside them are the pieces of work you can actually do.

---

## Scene 03

[natural pace] [interest] [natural]
Imagine the solution is to run three awareness sessions for high school students. One main step could be “prepare the content.” That can become smaller tasks: collect reliable information, choose the key messages, prepare simple slides, and check the materials. Another main step could be “arrange the session,” which may include finding a suitable place, checking permission, choosing a time, and preparing what the participants need. [short pause] Once the work becomes smaller, it becomes easier to see what is missing.

---

## Scene 04

[natural pace] [positive] [natural]
A practical plan usually answers a few simple questions. What needs to be done? What resources are needed? What will those resources cost? Who is responsible for doing or supporting the task? When should it happen? And where should it happen? [short pause] These questions connect the idea to reality. If one of them is missing, the plan may still look good on paper, but execution can become difficult later.

---

## Scene 05

[natural pace] [curiosity] [natural]
Resources are not only money. A resource can be a room, a laptop, printed material, transport, a contact person, information, or time. Cost means checking what those resources may require financially instead of discovering it too late. [short pause] For each task, ask: what do I need for this to happen? Then ask: do I already have it, can I access it, or would I need another option? This simple check can reveal problems before they become expensive or stressful.

---

## Scene 06

[natural pace] [interest] [natural]
Time and place also shape the plan. A task may be simple but still fail if it is scheduled too late or in the wrong place. So give important tasks a realistic time, and think about which tasks must happen before others. [short pause] Then check the location. Is it suitable for the people involved? Is it available? Does it support what the activity needs? Planning is not guessing a perfect future. It is making reasonable decisions before action starts.

---

## Scene 07

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [natural]
Here is a quick way to test the idea. Take one main step from a sample initiative and ask six questions: what are the smaller tasks, what resources are needed, what may cost money, who needs to handle or support it, when should it happen, and where should it happen? [medium pause] You do not need to complete a full project plan now. The point is to notice how much clearer an idea becomes when you move from a broad intention to specific work.

---

## Scene 08

[slow pace] [positive] [natural]
So the main idea is simple: planning turns a solution into manageable work. Break the project into main steps, break those steps into tasks, then connect each task to resources, cost, responsibility, time, and place. [short pause] In the next lesson, we will look at the next problem: a plan can be complete and still be weak. We will learn how to check it, find unrealistic assumptions, and improve it before action begins. That’s it for today, and I’ll see you in the next lesson.
