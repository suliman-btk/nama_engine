## Scene 01

[natural pace] [curiosity] [natural]

Welcome back. Before we move forward, think about Lesson One for a moment.

What do you remember about the Comfort Zone, the Challenge Zone, and the Pain Zone? [short pause] Which one is where growth happens?

Hold your answer.

Today we begin Unit One for real, with the question underneath everything else we’ll do in this unit: who am I? [medium pause]

To answer that, we need to start with one word: identity. Identity is what makes something itself, and not something else.

## Scene 02

[natural pace] [interest] [natural]

Think about an ordinary object, like a wooden table. You can describe its shape, color, material, marks, and the way it was made.

Now think about a flag. A flag is more than colors and shapes. It carries meaning, history, and belonging.

Then think about someone close to you, like your mother. You can probably describe what makes her unique to you without needing a definition first.

Objects have identity. Places have identity. Communities have identity. And you have one too. [short pause]

---

## Scene 03

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [natural]

Now turn the question toward yourself.

Who are you? [medium pause] How would you describe yourself? What makes you different from other people your age, or other people who have similar backgrounds?

That question can be harder than it sounds.

Many of us spend a lot of time learning about other people and very little time learning how to describe ourselves.

Self-awareness begins when you can look at your own identity honestly, without exaggerating your strengths and without talking yourself down.

---

## Scene 04

[natural pace] [interest] [natural]

The source curriculum makes a simple point: people are unique in many ways, including things like fingerprints, eye patterns, and DNA.

But identity goes far beyond biology. [short pause] It includes the experiences, values, interests, abilities, beliefs, relationships, and environments that shape how you see yourself and how you move through the world.

A person who understands their identity can recognize their strengths, work on their weaknesses, and make better choices about where they can contribute.

---

## Scene 05

[natural pace] [serious] [natural]

One important rule before we go further: identity is not a ranking. [short pause]

The color of your skin, how you look, where you were born, where you live, or the community you belong to do not make you “better” or “worse” than someone else.

Differences are real, but difference is not superiority.

[medium pause] [determination] For a community leader, this matters. You will work with people whose backgrounds, interests, opinions, and experiences may be very different from yours. Respect starts with understanding that difference is normal.

---

## Scene 06

[natural pace] [curiosity] [natural]

Picture two photos. In the first, a group of very young children. In the second, a group of adults.

Which group would usually be easier for you to describe as distinct individuals? [medium pause]

The curriculum uses this comparison to make a point: as we grow, more layers of identity become visible through our experiences, choices, roles, interests, and relationships.

Identity is not one fixed label. Some parts stay with us, while other parts become clearer, stronger, or different as our lives change.

---

## Scene 07

[natural pace] [positive] [natural]

Now it’s your turn. Use the Personal Identity Wheel provided with this lesson, or draw a simple circle on paper.

Write your name in the center. Then write three words that describe you.

Around the wheel, add your favorite music, a skill you’re proud of, your favorite movie, food, book, hobby, color, and a personal motto or saying you try to live by.

Pause the video and take about five honest minutes. [medium pause] Don’t write what sounds impressive. Write what is true.

---

## Scene 08

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [natural]

Come back to your wheel and look at it as if you were meeting yourself for the first time.

Does one page capture your whole identity? [short pause] Of course not. That isn’t the point.

The point is that you just practiced something many people rarely do on purpose: you looked directly at yourself and put specific parts of your identity into words.

If you’re taking Act with other people, compare your wheels afterward. Notice what you share and what is different.

Neither similarity nor difference is a problem. Both help you understand people better.

---

## Scene 09

[natural pace] [determination] [natural]

Now think like a community leader. [short pause]

If you don’t understand your own values, strengths, interests, and limits, it becomes harder to understand what role you should take in a team or how you should respond when someone else sees things differently.

Knowing yourself does not mean becoming self-focused.

It gives you a stronger starting point for serving others, because you understand what you can offer, what you still need to develop, and where another person’s strengths may complement your own.

---

## Scene 10

[slow pace] [thoughtful] [serious]

The source curriculum closes this lesson by reminding us that developing ourselves should connect to benefiting other people.

The Prophet Peace Be Upon Him said: “The most beloved people to Allah are those who are most beneficial to people.” [medium pause]

Keep that idea with you.

Knowing who you are is not the final destination. It is preparation for what you will do with your abilities, your choices, and your time.

The strongest identity is not the one that looks impressive. It is the one you understand well enough to use responsibly.

---

## Scene 11

[natural pace] [hope] [natural]

Before we close, look back at the three words you would have used to describe yourself at the start of this lesson.

Would you keep the same three now? [short pause] Would you change one? Would you add something you had not noticed before?

Keep your Personal Identity Wheel. We will build on it.

Today you moved from the general question “who am I?” to a real first answer: my identity is made of many parts, and understanding those parts helps me understand what I bring to other people.

[positive] In the next lesson, we go from describing your identity to using self-awareness on purpose.
