## Scene 01

[natural pace] [interest] [natural]

Hi, and welcome back to Act. You now have three possible solutions in front of you. Nice. But we still have one important decision to make: which one actually fits best? [short pause] This is not about choosing the idea that sounds coolest or the one you personally like most. We need to look at each option from a few different angles first. A simple Solution Filter can help with that. Think of it as a way to slow the decision down, compare the ideas fairly, and make your reasons clearer. Keep your three ideas from the last lesson beside you, because we are going to use them all the way through this one.

---

## Scene 02

[natural pace] [curiosity] [natural]

The filter asks six simple questions. What strengths do you already have that could help with this solution? What could make it difficult for you? What useful skills could you build while doing it? Who could benefit, and does the idea really answer the need you found? What positive values could the solution support? And finally, is there a suitable place or situation where this idea can really work? [short pause] None of these questions decides the answer alone. The point is to look at the whole picture. A strong idea should make sense for the people, the need, your ability, and the situation around it.

---

## Scene 03

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [natural]

Start with your own ability and challenges. Remember the strengths and weaknesses you explored earlier in Act? Bring that thinking back here. Maybe one solution fits a skill you already have. Another may depend on something you have never done before. [short pause] Be honest about both sides. Your strengths can make a solution easier to start, while your challenges show where you may need more learning, support, time, or a simpler approach. This is not about proving that you can do everything alone. Good leadership includes knowing what you can handle now and where you still need to grow before taking responsibility for something bigger.

---

## Scene 04

[natural pace] [interest] [natural]

Next, look at the skills you wanted to build in the last lesson. Which solution gives you a real chance to practice them? Maybe one idea helps you improve communication. Another could build planning, budgeting, design, or a technical skill. [short pause] This matters because real projects can become a place to learn through doing. But keep the order right: the project should answer a real community need first. Your personal growth is a benefit, not the main reason for choosing the solution. So ask both questions together: could this idea help people, and could it also give me a useful chance to develop skills while doing the work responsibly?

---

## Scene 05

[natural pace] [serious] [natural]

Now focus on the people who could benefit. How many people could this solution realistically reach, and does it actually match the need you wrote in Lesson Nine? [short pause] Do not just write a big number because it looks impressive. Think about who would really use or receive the solution. If an idea can reach many people but does not solve the need, it is still a weak fit. If another idea reaches a smaller group but clearly answers the problem you studied, that information matters too. The filter is helping you compare real fit, not just appearance. Keep asking: who benefits, how do they benefit, and does this solution connect back to the need?

---

## Scene 06

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [natural]

There are two more checks. First, values: what positive value could this solution support through the way it works? Maybe it encourages responsibility, cooperation, learning, care, or another value connected to the project. Second, the environment: is there a suitable place or situation for this idea to work? [short pause] A good idea can fail if the setting does not support it. Think about access, the people involved, the place, and whether the solution can actually be carried out there. You do not need a perfect situation. You just need enough of a fit that the idea has a real chance to work instead of staying as a nice idea on paper.

---

## Scene 07

[natural pace] [positive] [natural]

Now use the Solution Filter worksheet with this lesson. Put your three ideas into the filter and answer the six questions for each one. Keep your answers short and clear. [medium pause] Take your time here. You are comparing: your strengths, your challenges, the skills you could build, the people who may benefit, the values the idea supports, and whether the environment fits. When you finish, place the three options side by side and look at the whole picture. Do not choose because one box looks good. Look for the solution that makes the strongest overall sense for the need, the people, and what you can realistically take forward.

---

## Scene 08

[natural pace] [determination] [natural]

Now make the choice. Which solution has the best overall fit? Write it down, then add two or three simple sentences explaining why you chose it. [short pause] And remember, the filter is a guide, not a cage. If your comparison makes you realize that another idea outside your original three would fit much better, you can bring that idea back and run it through the same filter before deciding. What matters is that the final choice has a clear reason behind it. You are moving away from “I like this idea” and toward “This idea fits the need, the people, my situation, and what I can actually do.”

---

## Scene 09

[slow pace] [hope] [natural]

That’s it for today. You started with three possible solutions and used a clear way to compare them instead of guessing. Keep your completed filter and the one solution you selected. [short pause] Next, we are going to make that solution much clearer and easier to work with. We will check whether it is specific, measurable, realistic in your situation, and clear about time. Then we will look at how a simple creative change can make the idea stronger without losing the real need behind it. Keep your final solution with you, because we will build directly on it next. I’ll see you in the next lesson.
