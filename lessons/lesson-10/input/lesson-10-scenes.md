## Scene 01

[natural pace] [interest] [natural]

Hi, and welcome back to Act. Now we can finally start thinking about solutions. You know the problem, and you have a clearer idea of what people need. The next step is not to choose one answer immediately. It is to create options. [short pause] A simple way to start is with the question, “How can I…?” For example, instead of saying, “I will make an awareness campaign,” ask, “How can I help people get the information they need?” That question gives your mind more room. Maybe the answer is a campaign, maybe it is a guide, a service, a meeting, a digital tool, or something much simpler. First, open the space. Choosing comes later.

---

## Scene 02

[natural pace] [curiosity] [natural]

This is where brainstorming helps. Brainstorming means putting down ideas before judging them too quickly. For a few minutes, your job is simply to think of as many answers as you can. Write the obvious ideas. Write the strange ones. Write ideas that may be too expensive or too difficult. [short pause] You are not promising to use any of them. You are just giving yourself choices. If you reject every idea the second it appears, you may stop before you reach something better. So keep two stages separate: first, create ideas. After that, check which ideas are realistic and useful. That small change can make your thinking much more creative.

---

## Scene 03

[natural pace] [positive] [natural]

Let’s do it. Write your “How can I…?” question at the top of a page. Then give yourself five minutes and write as many possible solutions as you can. One idea per line is enough. [medium pause] Try not to edit yourself while you are writing. When the five minutes are over, look at everything you have. Combine ideas that are almost the same. Then choose three that seem strongest. Use three simple checks: can I realistically do this, does it match the real need, and could it bring useful benefit? [short pause] Keep those three ideas. You are not choosing the final one yet. You are building a small shortlist that you can compare more carefully next.

---

## Scene 04

[natural pace] [interest] [natural]

Sometimes one good idea comes from mixing parts of several smaller ideas. Maybe one idea is training, another is a simple online tool, and another is improving a service that already exists. You may find that the best option uses a little from more than one of them. [short pause] That is why it helps to give ideas some space before cutting them down. The first idea is not always the best one, and the most exciting idea is not always the most useful one. What matters is finding something that fits the problem, the need, and what you can actually manage. Good ideas become stronger when you compare them, combine useful parts, and remove what does not help.

---

## Scene 05

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [natural]

There is another nice part about community projects: while you are helping, you can also build useful skills. Maybe your project needs communication, planning, social media, budgeting, organizing events, writing, design, or technical work. [short pause] Doing real tasks gives you a chance to practice those skills, not just read about them. Later, that experience may help you explain what you can do in a CV, portfolio, interview, or future project. Volunteering does not guarantee a job, of course. But it can give you real situations where you learn, make mistakes, improve, and understand how your skills work when other people are depending on you.

---

## Scene 06

[natural pace] [interest] [natural]

Imagine you want to work in marketing one day. You understand social media, but you have never planned a real campaign. A volunteer project could give you a small place to practice: set a goal, create posts, organize the work, watch the response, and learn what worked. Or maybe you want experience with finance. Helping a community organization with simple financial tasks, with the right guidance, can give you useful practice. [short pause] The point is not to work for free forever. The point is to notice that service can also become a place to grow. You can help with something meaningful while building experience that may be useful in your future.

---

## Scene 07

[natural pace] [thoughtful] [natural]

Now look at your three solution ideas. What skills would each one need? Be honest. Maybe one idea needs a skill you already have. Maybe another needs something you have never done before. That does not mean you have to throw the idea away. [short pause] It may mean you need to learn first, ask someone experienced for advice, choose a simpler version, or pick a different option. Knowing your limits is part of good leadership too. Confidence is useful, but pretending you can do something when you cannot can hurt the project and the people involved. It is completely fine to say, “I need to learn this before I take responsibility for it.”

---

## Scene 08

[natural pace] [positive] [natural]

Take a minute and write down at least three skills you would like to improve while working on your project. [medium pause] They could be communication, listening, planning, decision-making, problem-solving, leadership, budgeting, negotiation, or a practical skill connected to your idea. Choose skills that you will actually use. Do not pick something only because it sounds impressive. Then look at your three solution ideas again and ask which one gives you a real chance to practice those skills while still helping the people you care about. Your project should serve the community first, but it can also become a useful place for your own growth.

---

## Scene 09

[natural pace] [serious] [natural]

Before moving on, check the three ideas against the need you wrote earlier. For each one, ask: how exactly does this help? [short pause] If you cannot explain the connection in one or two simple sentences, the idea may not fit. Change it or remove it. Also check that your three ideas are really different. Three versions of the same idea do not give you much choice. Go back and create another option if you need to. This part is worth the effort. You are trying to avoid choosing an idea just because you like it. The final solution should make sense for the problem, the people, and what can realistically be done.

---

## Scene 10

[slow pace] [hope] [natural]

So now you have two useful things to carry forward: three possible solutions and a short list of skills you want to build. [short pause] You did not rush into one answer. You gave yourself choices, checked them against the real need, and kept the strongest options. That gives you a much better place to make a decision from. Next, we will compare those ideas more carefully and choose the one that fits best. The goal is not to find a perfect solution. It is to find one that is useful, realistic, connected to the real need, and possible to improve as you learn more. Keep your notes with you, because we will use them again in the next step. That’s it for today. Keep your three ideas and your skill list with you, and I’ll see you in the next lesson.
