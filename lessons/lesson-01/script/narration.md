# Lesson 1 — Welcome to ACT: Finding Your Challenge Zone

## 1. Opening Hook

Have you ever been asked to do something that felt a little scary — and said yes anyway? That feeling — nervous and excited at once — has a name. Over the next few minutes, you're going to learn what it's called, and why the program you're starting today is built entirely around it.

Welcome. You're about to begin something called ACT.

## 2. Welcome / Connection to ACT

Let's start the way this program is meant to start. {{arabic:basmala}}. Thank you for being here.

Here's something to know right away: this program isn't going to feel like a classroom. It's casual, it's informal, and you're allowed to be yourself in it. What you're starting is a series of exercises, activities, and games — bring real enthusiasm to it, because what you put in is what you'll get out.

This program is called ACT — which stands for **Attitude Cultivates Transformation**. That's exactly what it is: built on the idea that your attitude, how you choose to face things, is what actually transforms you.

ACT is designed to equip you with real skills for your future — the kind the job market actually asks for — through social and voluntary work. It was built specifically for you, matching your aspirations and the skills you need. And you've been chosen to be part of it, alongside other young people with the same bright future ahead of them.

It's worth knowing who's behind this. ACT is a global initiative by the **NAMA Foundation**, working alongside **Ghadan** — a not-for-profit consultancy and capacity-building foundation. Take a second with that phrase, "not-for-profit": an organization that takes no profit for its partners or stakeholders, aiming everything instead at its mission — increasing social participation in your community.

And there's something to look forward to: an awards ceremony, held across Tanzania, celebrating the strongest groups, with official guests in attendance. Everything you do here can lead somewhere real.

## 3. Main Concept 1 — What ACT Is Actually Built From

Here's the shape of the journey you're starting. ACT is built from four units, and each one answers a different question and builds different skills.

**Unit 1 is Self-Awareness.** This is where you are right now — getting to know yourself, your identity, your strengths and capabilities, through games and activities that are actually engaging.

**Unit 2 is Social Awareness.** You'll become aware of your society — its strengths and its limits — and what it means to be part of a collective community, watching examples from other societies along the way.

**Unit 3 is Awareness of Social Issues.** You'll become aware of the social issues around you, understand how serious they are, build a sense of social justice, and choose one issue you personally want to work on.

**Unit 4 is Social Initiatives.** This is the biggest unit — half the whole program, and often the most rewarding. You'll learn the real fundamentals of planning and managing a project, choose your own, build it, and share what you achieved.

Notice the shape of that journey: **who am I, who are others, what do others struggle with, and what can I offer?** That's the path ACT walks you through, one unit at a time.

## 4. Getting to Know Your Guide

Here's something trainers in this program love to do on day one: they share two true things about themselves, and one lie — and let the room guess which is which. It's a simple trick, but it works, because it turns "being taught at" into "getting to know someone."

So here's a challenge for you: think of two true things about yourself, and one believable lie. Keep them in your head — or better, write them down. You'll get a chance to try this out on someone in your life today.

That's the spirit this whole program runs on — it works best when it feels like something we're doing together. So — how excited are you to get started?

## 5. Activity — Getting to Know Yourself

Let's begin the way this program always begins: by breaking the ice. Grab a piece of paper and tear or cut it into three small pieces. On each piece, write one thing about yourself: a hobby, a skill, or a personal trait — one per piece. It doesn't have to be impressive, just true.

**Now it's your turn.** Pause the video, write your three things, and come back when you're ready.

*[If you're watching with a group: fold and mix the papers, have each person draw three at random, then go find who wrote each one and get to know them. If you're alone, keep your three papers — you'll build on this idea of "who am I" throughout the program.]*

## 6. Reflection

That's a small moment, but it matters more than it seems. You just put into words three real, specific things about who you are. Self-awareness — exactly what Unit 1 is about — starts with honest moments like this one.

## 7. Building Our Safe Space

Now let's talk about the kind of space we want this to be. What would you want from the people around you — and from me — to make this program feel worthwhile? What would help you feel comfortable enough to actually speak your mind here?

A few examples of what usually comes up: phones away except for real emergencies, showing up on time, and — maybe most important — nobody making fun of anyone else's ideas.

**If you're watching in a group:** talk through this together, agree on your own ground rules, write them down, and have everyone sign. **If you're alone:** pause here and write, in your own words, the two or three things you personally commit to.

Here's the important part: this isn't a list of rules handed down to you. You're the ones deciding how this program runs — that's what makes it a safe space.

## 8. Pre-Workshop Survey

One more thing before we move on: a short survey. There are no grades on this — it's simply a way to capture where your knowledge stands right now, so that by the end of this journey, you can look back and see how far you've come.

**Now it's your turn.** Pause the video, and rate yourself honestly, right now, before you know anything else about this program:
- I understand what ACT is and what it's for.
- I could name at least one of the four units in this program.
- I know what the "challenge zone" means.
- I feel confident stepping outside my comfort zone.
- I'm excited to start this program.

Answer honestly, keep your answers somewhere safe, and come back and press play when you're done. We'll come back to this at the very end of the program, so you can see how much has changed.

## 9. Main Concept 2 — The Three Learning Zones

Now we get to the heart of this lesson — an idea that's going to follow you through this entire program.

Picture three circles: your **Comfort Zone** ("Easy"), your **Challenge Zone** ("Let's Go"), and your **Pain Zone** ("No Way!").

The **Easy zone** is where you do things without needing to think hard or put in effort. The **Impossible zone** is where you run into something you genuinely don't know how to tackle, and it leaves you stuck. The **Let's Go zone** — the challenge zone — is where you have some idea how to tackle something, but not yet enough experience to do it easily. That's exactly what makes it a challenge.

## 10. Activity — Which Zone Would You Choose?

Five situations. For each one, picture yourself in it, and decide — honestly — which zone: Easy, Let's Go, or No Way.

**First:** you're on a trip with your teacher and classmates when the plane develops a technical problem, and the pilot tells everyone to jump with a parachute.

**Second:** you have to dive into the ocean where there are sharks — locals say these particular sharks are peaceful.

**Third:** you have to speak in front of 700 people.

**Fourth:** you're handed a strange meal with unknown ingredients.

**Fifth:** you have to voice your honest opinion in front of decision-makers who you know disagree with you.

**Pause the video** and decide, for each of the five, which zone you landed in — and if you can, think about why.

*[If watching in a group: after each scenario, have everyone move to a part of the room representing their zone, and ask a few to explain their choice.]*

## 11. Reflection — Talking It Through

What did you discover about yourself? Are the three zones clear now — comfort, challenge, pain? What's the real difference between them? And how does each one actually feel to you?

Why did some situations feel "easy" to you and not to someone else? Why did some feel like "Let's Go," others "impossible"? Think back on your own life — have times you felt genuinely challenged ever made you stronger? Does the comfort zone ever really grow your knowledge or power? Does the pain zone add anything at all? Does the challenge zone actually benefit and strengthen you?

So here's the real question: **which zone do we need to spend our time in, to keep growing and learning?** It's easy to assume the comfort zone, because it's comfortable. But the zone that actually grows your strengths — the ladder toward becoming a capable, influential person — is the challenge zone.

## 12. Final Reflection — Why the Challenge Zone Matters

God created us with the ability to develop and advance. The real difference between an exceptional human being and an average one isn't talent — it's how much they've used and benefited from that ability.

The Prophet, {{arabic:salawat-on-the-prophet}}, said: "Paradise is surrounded by hardships." Hardship is about as close as it gets to the challenge zone.

A person can grow — psychologically, spiritually, emotionally, even financially — just by taking one step: out of the comfort zone, into the challenge zone. Youth, the stage of life you're in right now, is the best period to learn, face challenges, and grow in strength, knowledge, and power — for your own future and your community's.

This exercise can genuinely change your life. Many of us are serving our community for the first time through this program — that will teach you more than you expect. Children think enjoyment comes from taking. But people who've actually lived know the truest pleasures are in giving, not taking.

This program — ACT — is built to deliberately put you in the challenge zone, again and again, so you walk out having genuinely added something new to who you are.

## 14. Reflection — After the Video

What did you see happen? How did people react when that field was first built — and why do you think their reaction changed once the team started playing? How did it feel watching them win?

Why do you think those boys were able to beat so many more established teams — and keep playing hard through bad weather?

Here's the truth underneath that story: if it hadn't been for the harsh ground and the pain they pushed through, they'd never have developed the skill to win. Hardship is what builds strong people. A genuinely tough situation isn't a sign something's gone wrong — it's a sign you're growing.

That team went on to win seven championships in a row. Facing a challenge can be hard at the start, and yes, you might even be mocked for it — the way those boys were mocked for playing on a wooden field on an island. But the same people who mocked them became their biggest supporters once they saw the skill and self-belief behind it.

If you want to lead in your community, hold onto that mindset. Whenever a challenge comes your way, ask: *what can I learn from this? What skill comes out the other side?* Even everyday challenges — like working to earn money — are quietly teaching you skills the job market values everywhere.
